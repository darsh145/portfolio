AthlezTech — Case Study
UX & Brand Design

Designing smarter
cricket training

A mobile app for real-time cricket performance analytics, paired to a smart wristband — built for grassroots players and coaches.

Role UX/UI & Brand Designer
Company AthlezTech
Timeline 3 months
Domain Sports-tech / Wearables
Overview

Context

AthlezTech is a sports-tech startup developing a smart wristband for athletes. The first product phase focused on cricket batsmen, capturing player movements through embedded sensors and surfacing them as actionable insights on mobile.

My work spanned the full product design — from UX flows and mobile UI to brand identity and an early waitlist website to build pre-launch awareness.

Team

Product Engineer Hardware Designer UI/UX Designer (me)
01

Understanding
the challenge

Problem

Traditional cricket coaching relies heavily on subjective feedback. Many talented players go unnoticed due to favoritism or lack of access to proper analytics tools.

Goal

Build an accessible app that syncs with the wristband, visualizes real-time performance stats, and provides objective insights to improve technique.

Constraints

Limited hardware budget Simple enough for grassroots players Wearable sensor integration
02

Research &
discovery

Market Research

I explored comparable apps — SmartCricket BatSense and Zepp Baseball — studying common metrics, visualization styles, and gaps in existing UX and accessibility.

Key Findings

Players wanted visual stances, not just numbers. Coaches needed simple player comparison. Most apps lacked localized, budget-friendly solutions.

Feature prioritization — MoSCoW

✦

Must have

Live stats — bat swing speed, angles, session graphs.

◈

Should have

Stance visualization via vectorized player animation.

⬡

Could have

Personalized improvement suggestions per session.

◎

Won't have (v1)

Social or community comparison features.

03

Designing
the experience

Key app flow

Mode selection Real-time device sync Shot-by-shot analytics Session progress graphs Improvement insights

The standout feature: a vectorized player animation that replicates the batsman's real stance, bat swing, and angle — letting both player and coach spot posture issues in real time, not just after the session.

Brand & identity work

Visual Identity

Designed the logo and typography system to establish a credible, sports-tech brand identity from the ground up.

Waitlist Website

Built an early landing page to attract initial users and generate awareness before the product launched.

04

Outcome &
impact

Stance visualization was the most used and praised feature among testers

Coaches reported easier player feedback and performance tracking

Early prototypes attracted attention from local cricket academies

This project pushed me to think beyond screens — how real-world motion translates to digital feedback, and how hardware constraints directly shape UX decisions.

Reflections

Key learnings

01

Hardware constraints are a design input, not just a limitation — understanding sensor capabilities early shaped every UX decision downstream.

02

Grassroots users need simplicity above all — complex analytics are only valuable if the interface makes them immediately understandable without instruction.

03

Visual feedback (the stance animation) resonated far more with users than numerical data — showing motion is more intuitive than measuring it.

What I'd improve next

Improved onboarding for first-time device users Social comparison dashboard for team insights
